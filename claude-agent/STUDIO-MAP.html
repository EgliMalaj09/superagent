Egli Studio Map

One master agent takes every goal, plans the route, and hands each piece to the studio that knows how to do it. You talk to one place; the work lands in the right folder.

Who answers to whom

How every job runs

This is the default order for anything bigger than a single step. The amber steps are where it stops and waits for you.

  1. You give a goalAlways in a session opened in claude-agent.
  2. Master proposes the routeGoal, steps, who does each, what runs in parallel, cost and time. You say go or change it.
  3. Briefs go outEach specialist gets your words, file paths, format, and the right knowledge files. Independent pieces run at once.
  4. Approval stops come back to youScript, cut list, prompts and their cost, scene renders. The master shows them; you answer; the same specialist continues.
  5. Outputs pass alongFinished files from one specialist are handed to the next one that needs them.
  6. You get the resultThe finished files and a short summary.
  7. Taste is savedWhat you liked or rejected is written down the same turn, so the next job starts smarter.

Example: one video, split across agents

Sunday's AI SHQIP Ep. 4 from your egli_ai plan needs your face video and a Claude-animated toolkit map. The cut and the animation don't depend on each other, so they run at the same time.

Folder plan on your Desktop

The studios stay where they are. The master folder only points at them, so each studio still works on its own when you open it directly for a long job.

Rules that keep it tidy

Always

  • Start in claude-agent, even for a quick question.
  • Nothing that costs money runs without your yes. Google calls show the prompt and the number of calls first.
  • Every new look gets a style card: animation styles in animation-library/references/, editing styles in claude-editor/styles/.
  • When two rules disagree, the newest one wins and the old line is edited.

Coming later

  • Director: story, shot list, blocking and a shared look bible. Works step by step with your approval.
  • Prompt director: turns one shot into a ready prompt. Many copies run at once, one per shot, at most 3–4 video jobs at a time.
